# Mechanism Diligence — review pack

Public pack for an outside review. These files are not on mechanismdiligence.com.

## The standard is ibuprofen v7

Ibuprofen v7 is the picture standard. It is the cinematic cut: a dark frame, 3D molecules, labels on the molecules, and one action per scene. Do not treat ibuprofen v4 as the standard. That later cut is a text-and-diagram film and is not in this pack.

Atorvastatin v4 is included only as a later schematic attempt. Judge it against ibuprofen v7. Omeprazole v3 is the newest test film. Judge that against ibuprofen v7 as well.

## What to send back

Separate critical mistakes from non-critical ones.

Critical: a false or overclaimed mechanism, a wrong dose or maximum, a mixed product or route, a half-life treated as the effect duration, a contraindication softened or dropped, a draft fact spoken as settled, or a picture that shows a different event from the sentence.

Non-critical: awkward speech, a weak picture, a repeated shot, a label in the wrong place, a missing scene, or a chapter that is too thin.

For each drug: the critical mistakes, the non-critical ones, one better scene for the actual inhibition step, and anything that must be checked against the current US label before filming. Do not rewrite a whole script. Give a replacement line only where a line is wrong or confusing.

Then, across the set: where atorvastatin v4 and omeprazole v3 drift from ibuprofen v7, which three drugs are the best next films, which scripts are not safe to film yet, and five reusable scene ideas described as shots.

Rules the films are trying to keep: one product and one route; the condition before the drug; a journey from route to organ to cell to partner to effect; a spoken scale change when only the camera moves; dose start and maximum as label limits; clocks kept apart; same-action harm kept; limits and the education line at the end; close to mechanismdiligence.com. No face, no mascot, no music under the science. Do not invent a transporter, enzyme, percent, or tissue the cited label does not name. The picture should look like ibuprofen v7: molecules you can watch act, not a slide of text.
