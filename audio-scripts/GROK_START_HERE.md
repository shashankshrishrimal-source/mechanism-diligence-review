# Grok: make the three-depth videos

This is the current entry point for **Basic (Non-Medical terms), Medical and Scientific** production. Read [the level index](README.md), choose the requested medicine and level, then use that exact script. The package contains 19 medicines at each of three depths.

## First production pass

For the new adaptations, start with **ibuprofen Basic**, then **ibuprofen Medical**. Use the existing Scientific work and reusable shots where appropriate. Keep each level in a separate output folder; do not overwrite earlier films or production assets.

1. Watch the actual [cinematic ibuprofen v7](../films/ibuprofen-v7.mp4), the corrected approximately 2 MB file. It is the picture standard, not a transcript to copy. Follow its black-frame molecular look and make the causal events visibly move.
2. Read the selected level's README. Use `V2_Basic/scripts/ibuprofen.txt` or `V2_Medical/scripts/ibuprofen.txt` as the spoken master. The `.txt` contains all and only the words to speak, including questions. Use the matching file for every later drug.
3. Read the drug's section in [V2_Scientific_Evidence.md](V2_Scientific/V2_Scientific_Evidence.md), the selected level's EVIDENCE.md, and [the adaptation record](V2_Adaptation_Evidence.md). These control source scope and qualifications. Current-label clarifications in the adaptation record must be checked across levels; do not copy a weaker earlier safety statement into a new film.
4. Recheck the exact selected US product label before recording. If a conflict remains, identify the sentence, source and proposed correction. Hold that sentence while continuing unaffected work. Do not invent a dose, mechanism, reviewer or approval.

## What changes between levels

**Basic** is the shortest, with everyday language and approximately fifth-grade comprehension. Keep the cause-and-effect chain clear. Explain any retained medical term when it first appears. Do not add technical detail from Scientific just because it is available. Basic deliberately omits numeric dose schedules; do not place a Medical dose card over Basic narration. A card may name the route and formulation and direct the viewer to the exact product label.

**Medical** is shorter than Scientific and focuses on clinically useful mechanisms, meaningful uncertainty, adverse-effect links and relevant adult label context. Use clinical terms where useful and define terms needed to follow the explanation. Do not restore atomic contacts or research digressions removed during adaptation.

**Scientific** remains the detailed source. Its separate guide applies to Scientific production only. All three share the same underlying science; shortening must not make the mechanism more certain, more selective or safer than the evidence supports.

## Build the film

Create a sentence-aligned storyboard and sourced shot list. For each shot record the exact spoken words, location, scale, visible event, edit-added labels, source/label section, uncertainty and estimated timing. Establish normal function, then show the drug acting and the consequence. Give the actual target interaction the most attention.

Render a short mechanism test, check it against the sentence and source, then continue to the complete review cut. Reuse a shot only when its action matches the current narration. Adjust timing and labels for the level. Do not create a shorter film by speeding up a longer voice track or showing unexplained Scientific detail.

For ibuprofen, show substrate approach and conversion before drug occupancy. Then show reversible noncovalent binding that reduces productive access, fewer downstream products, and a separate dissociation event. Do not show permanent attachment, enzyme destruction or every enzyme switching off. Keep the selected immediate-release oral tablet and adult OTC Advil context.

Use a natural US English teacher's voice in a quiet room. Aim for **115–125 words per minute for Basic** and **120–130 for Medical and Scientific**, with picture-led pauses. Do not speak titles, source notes or file names. Keep the full supplied opening and ending, including the statement that clinical review is pending. Check pronunciation by listening to the actual audio.

## Shared visual rules

- **1920×1080, full-bleed black, cinematic 3D.** Show molecules acting. Generate no letters or text inside the plates. Add readable labels afterward in each molecule's color; names must remain understandable without color alone.
- One explicitly labeled route and selected product per film. Oral is not the default. Albuterol is oral inhalation, adalimumab is subcutaneous, and esketamine is intranasal.
- Mark scale and location changes. Do not invent continuous body-to-atom travel, first pass, a stomach or blood trip, literal speeds, or unsupported protein parts.
- Separate distinct mechanisms into repeated target/action/consequence sequences. A downstream effect is not automatically a second direct target.
- Put supported benefit and harm from the same action in a shared frame. Do not manufacture a causal explanation for every side effect.
- Retain uncertainty in voice and picture. Mark proposed pathways throughout their sequence. Keep animal-study detail out of narration.
- Keep onset, blood half-life, duration of action and clinical benefit separate. Dose statements remain adult label examples, never a personal recommendation. Never soften a contraindication.
- No mascot, patient face, joke, music under the science or happy-ending flourish.

## Deliver

Deliver a review MP4, narration audio, captions, storyboard, sourced shot list, still/motion prompts, clean plates and editable project assets. Include a short list of unresolved scientific, label, visual or pronunciation issues. Keep independent clinical review marked as pending. This handoff requests production review cuts; it does not authorize publishing final films to the website.

If your tools cannot open a source, watch a video or render a film, state that specific limitation and produce the concrete assets available to you. Do not claim to have watched or rendered material you could not access.
