# V2 Medical and Basic — adaptation evidence

**Not narration.** Prepared October 25, 2026. These are shorter adaptations of V2_Scientific, with selected-product US label rechecks and preserved qualifications. Independent clinical review remains pending. The [Scientific evidence companion](V2_Scientific/V2_Scientific_Evidence.md) retains the full named bibliography, source-access limitations and laboratory-versus-human distinctions. This adaptation did not repeat the complete literature review or establish new clinical validation.

## Cross-level label clarifications

- **Metformin:** the selected current label §5.1 goes beyond listing mitochondrial disease as a risk factor: use is not recommended in mitochondrial diseases such as MELAS or MIDD. Medical states that restriction explicitly; Basic describes diseases of cells' energy-making parts. The older Scientific audio wording is preserved as source history and must not override this clarification during production. The same label advises avoiding use in hepatic disease and warns against excessive alcohol intake. [Selected US label, §§5.1 and 8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5647230e-5bc6-1802-e063-6394a90a2724).
- **Esketamine:** the adaptations include the selected Spravato label's instruction to avoid driving until the next day after a restful sleep. Minimum observation time does not by itself establish readiness to leave. The per-drug note below identifies the source and section.

## What was simplified

Medical keeps clinical mechanisms and meaningful dose context while removing atomic contacts and research digressions. Basic removes numeric dose schedules and most intermediate names; it keeps the causal sequence, major harm links, serious restrictions and uncertainty. Omitting a schedule is deliberate, not a claim that the label lacks a dose. Neither film replaces the full product label or teaches personal treatment decisions.

Basic uses eight spoken questions; Medical keeps the original eleven. The shared opening and closing are unchanged. No animal experiments are added to narration. Definitions and short sentences support the approximately fifth-grade Basic target; see [the language report](READABILITY.md) for measured scores and limits.
