# Adalimumab

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence.
Let’s talk about adalimumab, which is the generic name.
Humira is a brand name, and it may be the name you already know.

## What is this medicine?

We’re following Humira for adults with rheumatoid arthritis that is moderate to severe. In this illness, the body’s defenses attack joints. Humira is an antibody: a protein made to bind one target.

## How does it get to work?

Humira is a shot under the skin. It moves through vessels that drain fluid into blood. It then reaches spaces around cells. Its main work happens outside cells.

## How does it work?

Cells send messages to one another. One message protein is called TNF. It binds to spots on the outside of cells. These spots are proteins called receptors.

TNF can keep a chain of signals going. In this disease, that can cause joints to swell and become damaged.

Humira binds to TNF. That leaves less TNF free to bind its receptors. The drug catches the message before it reaches a cell. Fewer defense cells are then called into the joint.

Other signals still work. Blocking TNF does not shut off all the body’s defenses.

## How can it help and cause harm?

Fewer TNF signals can reduce joint swelling and slow damage. But TNF also helps keep germs in check. Blocking it can let serious infections develop or return.

The strongest label warning covers serious infections and cancer. Some can be deadly.

## What is still unclear?

We know Humira binds TNF and blocks its signals. It may affect cells in other ways too. We do not know how much each extra action helps. It does not kill every cell involved in joint swelling.

## How long does it work?

The drug can stay in the body for weeks. Cells slowly break it down. The body may also make proteins that bind to the drug. These can change how well it works. How long it stays does not tell us how long relief lasts.

## What should we know about doses and risks?

The plan for these shots depends on the illness and other drugs. This example is for the joint disease in adults.

Do not start during an active infection. Tests check for tuberculosis, or TB, which can hide in the body. Hidden TB needs treatment before Humira starts. Checks continue during treatment.

A serious infection requires stopping treatment. A severe allergic reaction requires an immediate stop and urgent care. A past hepatitis B infection can return. Vaccines that contain live germs should be avoided.

## What is the main idea?

Humira blocks a message that can harm joints. That same message also helps the body fight infection.
