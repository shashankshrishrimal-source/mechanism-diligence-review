# V2_Medical — Medical

Prepared 2026-10-25. This package adapts all 19 [V2_Scientific scripts](../V2_Scientific/V2_Scientific.md) for **clinicians, pharmacists and nurses**. Anyone may choose this level.

A shorter clinical explanation: retain the drug–target action, the clinically useful downstream effects, major benefit/harm links, important uncertainty, and the selected adult label context. Define terms needed to follow the causal chain. Leave atomic contacts and secondary research detail to Scientific.

## Give Grok these files

1. [Shared production guide](../GROK_START_HERE.md) — select the correct level before production.
2. [Complete Medical master](V2_Medical.md) — all 19 scripts.
3. [Individual scripts](scripts/) — `.txt` files contain only words to speak; `.md` files add headings for editing. Questions are spoken.
4. [Evidence and source mapping](EVIDENCE.md) — non-spoken source links, safety clarifications and limits.
5. [Timing estimates](TIMING.md) — approximate durations with modest pauses.

## Voice and pacing

Use natural US English at approximately **120–130 words per minute**. Speak as a real teacher in a quiet room. Pause after questions and when the picture needs time to show a change. Vary sentence rhythm naturally. Keep drug and brand pronunciations accurate; use the pronunciation notes in the Scientific evidence companion. Do not read citations, file names or editorial notes aloud.

Keep the supplied opening and entire closing, including **clinical review is pending**. The shared opening and closing are locked wording; they are not rewritten to lower a readability score. Simplifying an explanation must not turn an uncertain mechanism into a fact or a contraindication into a suggestion.

## Scope and dose choices

Each script retains its Scientific source's selected product, route and dosing indication. Medical retains a bounded adult label example where useful. Basic omits numeric dose schedules so its short explanation does not become an incomplete dosing instruction; it keeps the relevant formulation and serious restrictions. No script supplies a personal dose. The source's numeric dose information remains available in Medical/Scientific and the linked label.

The mechanisms remain the main subject. The Basic explanation does not imply that the medicine finds only diseased tissue, that a binding event permanently destroys a target, or that one known target completely explains an unresolved human benefit. A shorter script may omit a subsidiary mechanism, but it must not contradict it.

## Pictures

Use [cinematic ibuprofen v7](../../films/ibuprofen-v7.mp4) as the visual standard. Keep 1920×1080, full-bleed black, cinematic 3D and molecules visibly acting. No text is baked into plates; add each molecule's label in its own color afterward. Reuse scientifically compatible shots across levels, then retime and relabel them to match each narration. Do not merely speed up Scientific narration or retain a picture whose action is no longer explained.

Show normal function before the drug's action, followed by the consequence. Keep separate mechanisms in separate loops. A source-supported benefit and harm from the same action share a frame. Mark scale/location changes and proposed pathways explicitly. Never invent a route, first pass, blood trip, atomic detail or time course.

No mascot, patient face, joke, music under the science or happy-ending flourish. Keep animal-study detail in evidence notes. No translation is requested; these are three English explanation depths.

## Review status

These are editorial adaptations with source and language checks. They are not independently clinically validated, and no audience comprehension study has been performed. Recheck the selected current US label before recording. If it conflicts with a line, identify the sentence and source and resolve that line before recording it. Continue unaffected production work. Keep the pending-review statement in review cuts.
