# Famotidine

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence.
Let’s talk about famotidine, which is the generic name.
Pepcid is a brand name, and it may be the name you already know.

## What is this drug?

Famotidine suppresses stomach acid by blocking histamine H-two receptors. We’ll follow prescription oral tablets for adult symptomatic nonerosive GERD: reflux symptoms without erosive injury. Products combining famotidine with antacids are different formulations.

## What biological problem does it address?

Reflux exposes the esophagus to stomach contents. Symptoms do not require acid overproduction.

Nearby cells release histamine, which stimulates H-two receptors on acid-producing parietal cells. The receptor controls a signal. The proton pump performs the final acid-transport step.

## How does it reach its target?

After absorption from the gut, blood delivers famotidine to the stomach lining. Its receptor target is on the tissue-facing side of the parietal-cell membrane. The drug does not need to enter the stomach cavity, neutralize existing acid, or undergo acid-dependent activation.

## What happens at the target?

When histamine binds its receptor, a stimulatory G protein activates adenylyl cyclase. That enzyme makes cyclic AMP, an intracellular messenger. Cyclic AMP activates protein kinase A, helping stimulate acid secretion.

Famotidine competes with histamine for the receptor’s binding pocket and prevents effective activation. This makes it a competitive antagonist. It does not destroy histamine or permanently modify the proton pump.

## How does that action change the system?

Less histamine-driven signaling means less acid secretion. Histamine also interacts with gastrin and nerve signals, so blockade can reduce meal-related as well as nighttime secretion.

Other control pathways remain. Famotidine reduces the stimulation of pumps; it does not directly block the pumps’ chemical cycle or stop every source of acid secretion.

## What is established, and what is still uncertain?

H-two blockade is established. Acid suppression can weaken with repeated use, a phenomenon called tachyphylaxis. The exact adaptation responsible remains unresolved. Receptor upregulation should not be presented as the single proven explanation.

## How does the action connect to benefit and harm?

Lower acidity can relieve acid-related symptoms. The same pH change can reduce absorption of medicines that need an acidic stomach. Higher pH means less acidity.

Confusion and cardiac electrical disturbances are separate concerns, especially when impaired kidney clearance allows drug accumulation. They are not explained simply by reduced stomach acid.

## How long does the action last?

Label studies found acid suppression within an hour, lasting about ten to twelve hours after twenty- or forty-milligram doses. These are acid measurements, not guaranteed symptom-control times.

Kidney elimination strongly influences exposure. Reduced clearance and repeated-dose tolerance are distinct issues.

## What adult dosing does the label describe?

For symptomatic nonerosive GERD in adults weighing at least forty kilograms with normal kidney function, the prescription tablet label describes twenty milligrams twice daily for up to six weeks. Moderate or severe kidney impairment requires adjustment. This is an indication-specific regimen, not a personal dose or universal maximum.

## What must not be overlooked?

Do not use famotidine after a serious hypersensitivity reaction to it or another H-two receptor antagonist. Symptom improvement does not rule out stomach cancer. Review kidney function and interactions, including medicines whose absorption depends on acidity.

## What is the take-home mechanism?

Famotidine blocks histamine’s receptor signal upstream of acid secretion. It reduces stimulation of the proton pump, without neutralizing existing acid or covalently disabling the pump.
