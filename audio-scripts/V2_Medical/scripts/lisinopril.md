# Lisinopril

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence.
Let’s talk about lisinopril, which is the generic name.
Zestril is a brand name, and it may be the name you already know.

## What is this drug?

We’re following oral lisinopril tablets for adult hypertension. Lisinopril inhibits angiotensin-converting enzyme, or ACE. Two substrate reactions explain much of its benefit and harm: angiotensin-two formation and bradykinin breakdown.

## What biological problem does it address?

The kidneys release renin, starting a sequence that produces angiotensin one. ACE converts this into angiotensin two.

Angiotensin two activates AT-one receptors, tightening arteries and stimulating aldosterone release. Aldosterone promotes sodium retention and potassium excretion. Persistent signaling can sustain high blood pressure.

## How does it reach its target?

After intestinal absorption, lisinopril circulates as an active drug. It needs no liver activation. Much of its target is outside cells, including ACE on the lining of blood vessels.

## What happens at the target?

ACE uses zinc to help cut peptide bonds. Lisinopril binds reversibly in its active site and interacts with that zinc. It interferes with substrate processing without destroying ACE.

First, less angiotensin one becomes angiotensin two through ACE. Now follow bradykinin, a peptide that can widen vessels and increase their permeability. ACE normally breaks it down. Lisinopril slows that breakdown, allowing its signaling to persist.

## How does that action change the system?

Reduced angiotensin-two signaling relaxes arterioles and reduces aldosterone release. Vascular resistance falls, and renal sodium and potassium handling changes.

Removing angiotensin two’s restraint on renin can raise renin activity despite lower pressure. That rise alone does not mean treatment failure.

## What is established, and what is still uncertain?

ACE inhibition is established. Other enzymes can still produce angiotensin two, so lisinopril does not eliminate it. Bradykinin can support vessel relaxation, but its exact contribution to treatment benefit remains uncertain.

## How does the action connect to benefit and harm?

The same pressure reduction can cause dizziness or fainting. Less aldosterone can raise blood potassium, called hyperkalemia.

When kidney filtration depends strongly on angiotensin-two signaling, blocking that support can worsen kidney function. Monitor renal function and potassium.

Impaired bradykinin breakdown is implicated in dry cough and angioedema, deep tissue swelling that can threaten breathing. Individual susceptibility remains incompletely understood.

## How long does the action last?

Label studies found pressure lowering starting around one hour, peaking around six hours, and remaining measurable at twenty-four hours. These are pressure responses, not enzyme-occupancy measurements. Absorbed drug leaves unchanged in urine.

## What adult dosing does the label describe?

For hypertension, the usual start is ten milligrams once daily, or five with a diuretic. The usual range is twenty to forty milligrams daily. Eighty has been used without greater apparent benefit. Kidney impairment can require a lower start. These are label directions, not a personal dose.

## What must not be overlooked?

Do not use lisinopril after ACE-inhibitor-related angioedema or hypersensitivity, or with hereditary or idiopathic angioedema. It is contraindicated with neprilysin inhibitors. Allow at least thirty-six hours between lisinopril and sacubitril–valsartan in either direction. Do not combine it with aliskiren in diabetes.

Its boxed warning concerns fetal injury and death: discontinue as soon as pregnancy is detected. Tongue or throat swelling requires emergency care and immediate discontinuation.

## What is the take-home mechanism?

Lisinopril reduces ACE-dependent angiotensin-two formation while slowing bradykinin breakdown. Following both reactions links its blood-pressure effect to important kidney, potassium, cough, and swelling risks.
