## 2. What is rheumatoid arthritis?

Rheumatoid arthritis is a condition where the immune system drives inflammation in the joints.
TNF-alpha is one of the signals that helps that inflammation continue.
Joints can become swollen, painful, and damaged over time.
A clinician confirms the diagnosis.
This film follows the TNF signal while the drug is taken.

Scene: joint with inflammation. No drug. No face.

## 3. What does the drug do?

Adalimumab is a TNF blocker.
It is a monoclonal antibody that binds TNF-alpha.
It stops TNF-alpha from attaching to the p55 and p75 receptors on cells.
Less TNF signaling can reduce joint inflammation and slow damage while you take it.
It does not repair existing joint damage.

Scene: antibody binding TNF.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

It begins as a subcutaneous injection under the skin.
It is absorbed into the blood.
It circulates and meets TNF-alpha in the blood and in inflamed joints.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: injection then blood then joint.

## 5. What does it block?

TNF-alpha binds to receptors on cells and helps drive inflammation.
Adalimumab binds TNF-alpha and keeps it from attaching to those receptors.
Nothing is aiming. The shape fits. The block lets go when the complex is cleared.
With less free TNF-alpha, the inflammatory signal is reduced.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.
It does not bind TNF-beta.

## 6. What changes?

Joint inflammation can decrease.
Pain and swelling can ease.
The risk of further joint damage can fall while the drug is present.
Clinical improvement can take weeks.
It works while you take it every other week.

Scene: reduced joint inflammation.

## 7. How does it leave?

Adalimumab is a large protein.
It is cleared mainly by the body’s protein disposal systems.
The terminal half-life is about 10 to 20 days.
That half-life is not how long the clinical effect lasts for every person.
Peak levels after a single injection are not when the joint change is measured.

Scene: leave path.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

For adult rheumatoid arthritis the usual dose is 40 milligrams every other week by subcutaneous injection.
Some people not taking methotrexate may use 40 milligrams every week or 80 milligrams every other week.
These are label directions, not a dose chosen for you.
It is given under the skin.

Scene: dose card. 40 mg every other week usual.

## 9. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to adalimumab.
There is a boxed warning about serious infections and about malignancy.
The same TNF blockade that reduces joint inflammation can lower the ability to fight infections.
Live vaccines are not recommended while taking it.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources and feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
