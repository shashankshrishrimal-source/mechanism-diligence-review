## 2. What is a tight airway?

Bronchospasm means muscle around the airway tightens and the airway narrows.
Labeled uses: reversible obstructive airway disease and prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm.
Not every cause of breathlessness will respond.
Shapes in this film are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

Scene: narrowed bronchiole, copper muscle, small lumen. No face. No drug.

## 3. What can that narrowing do?

Narrowed airways can bring wheeze, chest tightness, cough, and harder airflow.
Albuterol is a reliever for that tightening.
It does not replace an inhaled corticosteroid.

Scene: same narrowed airway.

## 4. What does the drug do?

Albuterol is a short-acting beta two agonist.
It is a bronchodilator.
It is not a steroid or a cure for asthma.

Scene: gold molecule only.

## 5. How does it reach that muscle?

It is inhaled through the mouth into the airways.
Recommended doses give low blood levels, but some drug reaches the circulation.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.
No transporter is named in the sources used here.

Scene: branching airway with gold mist, then closer wall view.

## 6. What happens at the receptor?

On airway smooth muscle, albuterol activates beta two receptors.
That increases adenylyl cyclase activity and cyclic AMP.
Higher cyclic AMP is linked to relaxation of the bronchial muscle.
The label stops at cyclic AMP and relaxation.
Selectivity is relative. Beta two receptors also occur in the heart.

Scene: teal receptor, gold ligand, then violet cyclase and icy cAMP. No invented calcium channel.

## 7. What changes in the airway?

The airway can open so air moves more easily.
Among adult and adolescent responders, mean time to a 15% rise in FEV1 was about 5.4 minutes.
Mean duration in those trials was around 4 hours.
A dose often brings relief for up to 4 to 6 hours.
The plasma half-life is not how long the relief lasts.

Scene: open bronchiole, wide lumen.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

Each actuation delivers 90 micrograms of albuterol base.
The label describes 2 inhalations every 4 to 6 hours.
For some people, 1 inhalation every 4 hours is enough.
For exercise, 2 inhalations 15 to 30 minutes beforehand.
There is no single daily maximum in the sources used here.

Scene: dose card.

## 9. What are the limits?

Contraindicated after hypersensitivity to albuterol sulfate or to HFA-134a.
If breathing worsens right after a puff, stop and get urgent help. That can be paradoxical bronchospasm.
Too much can bring marked tremor, a fast or irregular heartbeat, or chest pain.
Beta two stimulation can shift potassium into cells and lower the blood level. That is a same-action harm.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
