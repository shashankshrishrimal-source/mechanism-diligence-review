## 2. What is high blood pressure?

High blood pressure means the force against vessel walls stays too high for too long.
You cannot feel the number.
Only a cuff or monitor confirms it.
Over years it strains the heart, vessels, and kidneys.

Scene: vessel walls under pressure. No drug.

## 3. What does the drug do?

Amlodipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker.
It relaxes blood vessels.
The heart pumps against less resistance.
Blood pressure falls.
It does not significantly change heart rate at usual doses.

Scene: vessel relaxing.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

It begins as a swallowed tablet.
It reaches the blood vessels.
It acts directly on vascular smooth muscle.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: tablet then vessel wall.

## 5. What does it block?

Smooth muscle cells have L-type calcium channels.
Calcium enters and helps the muscle contract.
Amlodipine sits in the channel and slows that entry.
With less calcium, the muscle relaxes.
The vessel widens.
The effect is greater on vessels than on heart muscle at usual doses.

Scene: L-type channel, gold drug seated. Teal channel.

## 6. What changes?

Vessels relax.
Blood pressure falls.
The full drop can take days to weeks.
It works while you take it once a day.
Peak blood concentration and the full blood-pressure response are different measurements.

Scene: relaxed vessel.

## 7. How does it leave?

It is extensively metabolized in the liver.
Metabolites leave mainly in urine and feces.
The terminal half-life is about 30 to 50 hours.
That half-life is not how long the pressure effect lasts.

Scene: leave path.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

Usual start is 5 milligrams once a day.
Maximum is 10 milligrams once a day.
Some people start at 2.5 milligrams.
These are label limits, not a dose chosen for you.

Scene: dose card.

## 9. What are the limits?

Do not use after a hypersensitivity reaction to amlodipine. Confirm the label’s wording before filming. Do not narrow this to “serious allergy” unless the label says that.
Ankle or leg swelling can occur and is more common at higher doses.
That swelling comes from the same vessel relaxation.
Dizziness is possible, especially when standing.
This film does not show your personal risk.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources and feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.

Scene: limits card.
