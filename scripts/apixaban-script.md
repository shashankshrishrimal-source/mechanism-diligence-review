# Apixaban — locked spoken script

Product: adult Eliquis oral tablets. Other formulations are a different film.

Spoken lines one sentence each. Do not say it dissolves an existing clot.

## 1. What is this drug?

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence.
You might be here to understand a drug you were prescribed, or to learn how it works.
This one is apixaban.
Eliquis is the brand name, and it may be the name you already know.
This film follows adult oral Eliquis tablets.

Scene: black. Pure-white MD mark, top left, not stretched, D open. “Mechanism Diligence” under the mark, smaller. Centered header “Mechanism Diligence” only on .

## 2. What is a harmful clot?

In atrial fibrillation the heart can form a clot that travels.
In the veins a clot can form in the leg and travel to the lung.
You may not feel the clot forming.
Tests or symptoms confirm it.
A clot can block blood flow.

Scene: vessel or heart chamber with a forming clot. No face. No drug. Source: labeled indications.

## 3. What does the drug do?

Apixaban is a factor Xa inhibitor.
Factor Xa helps turn prothrombin into thrombin.
Thrombin helps fibrin form a mesh that traps cells into a clot.
Apixaban blocks factor Xa, so less thrombin is made and fewer clots form.
It does not dissolve a clot that is already there.

Scene: clotting cascade schematic, Factor Xa blocked. Source: label 12.1.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

It begins as a swallowed tablet.
It is absorbed into the blood.
There it meets factor Xa, both free in the blood and attached to a forming clot.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: tablet, then blood with Factor Xa. Source: label 12.1.

## 5. What does it block?

Factor Xa sits in the clotting cascade.
Apixaban binds it directly and reversibly.
Nothing is aiming. The shape fits. The block lets go.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.
With less factor Xa activity, thrombin generation falls.
Less fibrin forms.
The clot is less likely to grow or start.
Platelets still work.
The drug does not block them directly.

Scene: Factor Xa with gold drug bound, less thrombin downstream. Teal Factor Xa. Source: label 12.1.

## 6. What changes?

The risk of certain clots falls while you take it.
The effect continues only while the medicine is on board.
Stopping suddenly can raise the risk of a clot.
It works for the labeled uses while taken as directed.

Scene: reduced clot formation in vessel. Source: label 12.1 and indications.


## 7. How does it leave?

Apixaban leaves in both urine and feces.
About 27 percent of total clearance is through the kidneys.
The apparent half-life is about 12 hours.
That half-life is not how long the clotting effect lasts.
The effect can persist for at least 24 hours after the last dose.

Scene: simple leave path. One picture.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

For nonvalvular atrial fibrillation the usual dose is 5 milligrams twice a day.
Some people take 2.5 milligrams twice a day. Do not film that rule until the label’s age, weight, and kidney criteria are written in full. “Age, weight, or kidney function” is not enough.
Other uses have their own dose plans.
These are label directions, not a dose chosen for you.
Take with or without food.

Scene: dose card. 5 mg twice daily usual for AF. Source: label 2.

## 9. What are the limits?

Do not use it if you have active bleeding.
Do not use it after a severe hypersensitivity to apixaban.
There are boxed warnings about stopping it early and about spinal procedures. Say the consequence in the warning, not only the subject. Do not film those lines until the label wording is in the script.
Bleeding can be serious.
Easier bleeding is the same action that reduces harmful clots.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources, and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.

Scene: limits card. Source: label 4, 5, boxed warning.
