## 2. What is treatment-resistant depression?

Treatment-resistant depression means major depressive symptoms that have not improved enough with at least two different antidepressants.
Symptoms can include low mood, loss of interest, or thoughts of suicide.
A clinician confirms the diagnosis and the prior treatments.
The drug is used with or without an oral antidepressant.

## 3. What does the drug do?

Esketamine is the S-enantiomer of ketamine.
It is a non-competitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor.
The label says the mechanism by which it exerts its antidepressant effect is unknown.
It can reduce depressive symptoms while you take it under supervision.
It does not replace other treatments.

It begins as a nasal spray.
It is absorbed through the nose and reaches the blood.
It crosses into the brain and meets NMDA receptors on nerve cells.

## 5. What does it block?

NMDA receptors are glutamate receptors on nerve cells.
Esketamine is a non-competitive antagonist at that receptor.

The main metabolite also has some activity at the same receptor, with less affinity.

## 7. How does it leave?

Esketamine is extensively metabolized in the liver, mainly by CYP2B6 and CYP3A4.
The main metabolite is noresketamine, and it is less active.
Most metabolites leave in the urine.
The terminal half-life is about 7 to 12 hours.
That half-life is not how long the clinical effect lasts.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

For treatment-resistant depression the induction dose is 56 or 84 milligrams twice a week for four weeks.
Maintenance is 56 or 84 milligrams once a week, then every one or two weeks.
These are label directions, not a dose chosen for you.
It is given as a nasal spray under supervision.

## 9. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to esketamine or ketamine.
There are boxed warnings about sedation, dissociation, abuse and misuse, and suicidal thoughts.
The same NMDA block that is linked to the effect can cause dissociation and sedation.
Blood pressure can rise.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources and feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
