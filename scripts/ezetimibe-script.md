## 2. What is high LDL?

Hyperlipidemia means too much fat-carrying material in the blood.
The part this film follows is LDL cholesterol.
LDL is a particle that carries cholesterol.
You cannot feel the number. Only a blood test confirms it.
If high LDL continues for years, cholesterol can build up in artery walls and narrow blood flow.
Shapes in this film are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

Scene: LDL particles in blood, then artery wall deposit. No drug.

## 3. What does the drug block?

Ezetimibe blocks a transporter called NPC1L1 on the brush border of the small intestine.
It pulls cholesterol from the gut into the body.
With it blocked, less cholesterol is absorbed.
The liver then takes more cholesterol back from the blood.
Ezetimibe does not block the liver’s cholesterol-making enzyme.

Scene: enterocyte brush border. Teal NPC1L1. Gold cholesterol. Purple ezetimibe seated.

## 4. What happens at the transporter?

Cholesterol from food and from bile meets NPC1L1.
Ezetimibe sits in the transporter and slows that entry.
More cholesterol stays in the gut and leaves in the stool.
In a two-week study, intestinal cholesterol absorption fell by about 54 percent compared with placebo.
That is a study average, not a promise for one person.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: closer brush border. Cholesterol held outside. No invented atom contacts.

## 5. What changes on the test?

Less cholesterol reaches the liver.
Hepatic cholesterol stores fall.
The liver clears more cholesterol from the blood.
LDL cholesterol falls.
The effect can begin within days to weeks.
It works while the 10 milligram dose is taken.
The plasma half-life is not how long the LDL drop lasts.

Scene: liver taking LDL particles back.

## 6. What dose does the label list?

The labeled dose is 10 milligrams once a day, with or without food.
There is no higher dose on this label.

Scene: one-line card. 10 mg once a day.

## 7. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to ezetimibe.
When used with a statin, follow the statin label for liver monitoring.
Liver enzyme increases can occur with the combination. Do not call that a same-action harm of the cholesterol pathway unless the label makes that link.
Ezetimibe is mainly eliminated in the bile after glucuronidation.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
