## 1. What is this drug?

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence.
You might be here to understand a drug you were prescribed, or to learn how it works.
This one is famotidine.
Pepcid is the brand name, and it may be the name you already know.
This film follows prescription oral tablets for adults.

## 2. What is excess stomach acid?

Excess acid can irritate the stomach or esophagus.
You may feel heartburn or pain.
Symptoms or tests confirm the need.
It is not a number you feel in the blood.

## 3. What does the drug block?

Famotidine is an H2 blocker.
It blocks histamine H2 receptors on the acid-making cells in the stomach.
With the receptor blocked, those cells make less acid.
Basal and stimulated acid fall.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

In the stomach lining, parietal cells have H2 receptors on their surface.
Histamine normally binds them and turns up the acid pump.
Famotidine sits in the receptor and reduces that signal.
Less acid is pumped into the stomach.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

## 5. What changes?

Acid output falls.
Symptoms of heartburn or ulcer can ease while you take it.
It works only while the medicine is present.
It does not heal every case by itself.
The drug leaves mainly in urine.

## 6. What dose does the label list?

Labeled adult doses are often 20 or 40 milligrams once or twice a day, depending on the use.
There is a maximum on the specific label.
These are label limits, not a dose chosen for you.

## 7. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to famotidine.
Reducing acid can raise the risk of certain infections or affect absorption of other medicines. That is the same action that eases heartburn.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
