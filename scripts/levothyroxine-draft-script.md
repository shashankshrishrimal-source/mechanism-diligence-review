## Chapter 1: What is low thyroid hormone?
What is hypothyroidism? The thyroid gland does not make enough thyroid hormone. Metabolism slows. You may feel tired, cold, or gain weight. A blood test for TSH and free T4 confirms it. Over time untreated low hormone can affect the heart and other systems.

## Chapter 2: What does levothyroxine do?
How does levothyroxine replace the hormone? Levothyroxine is synthetic T4, identical to the T4 the thyroid makes. It raises T4 levels. Most of the active hormone is T3, made from T4 in tissues.

## Chapter 3: The journey
It begins as a swallowed tablet. It is absorbed from the gut. It circulates and enters cells. Inside the nucleus, T4 and T3 bind thyroid receptors on DNA. That turns on gene transcription and protein synthesis. The body returns toward normal metabolic rate.

## Chapter 4: What changes
Energy use and many body processes improve while the dose is right. The effect is measured by blood tests, not by feeling alone. It works only while you take it. The dose is adjusted to the individual.

## Chapter 5: Dose
On the US label the dose is individualized. There is no single starting or maximum dose that fits everyone. The goal is a normal TSH for most people with primary hypothyroidism. Take it on an empty stomach, usually in the morning, and wait before food or other medicines. These are label principles, not a dose chosen for you.

## Chapter 6: Limits
Do not use for weight loss. There is a boxed warning against that use. Too much can cause heart strain, bone loss, or other problems. This film does not show your personal risk.
