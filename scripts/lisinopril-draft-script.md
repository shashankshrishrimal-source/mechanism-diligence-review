Welcome to Mechanism Diligence. You might be here to understand a drug you were prescribed, or to learn how it works. This one is lisinopril. Zestril and Prinivil are brand names you may know.

What is high blood pressure?
High blood pressure means the force of blood against vessel walls stays too high for too long. You cannot feel the number. Only a blood pressure cuff or monitor confirms it. Over years it strains the heart, vessels, and kidneys.

How does lisinopril work?
Lisinopril is an ACE inhibitor. It blocks the enzyme that turns angiotensin one into angiotensin two. Angiotensin two is a strong signal that tightens blood vessels. With less of it, vessels relax. The heart pumps against less resistance. It also lowers a hormone that holds salt and water.

The pathway.
In the body, renin from the kidney helps make angiotensin one. The ACE enzyme, mostly in the lungs and vessels, converts angiotensin one to angiotensin two. Lisinopril sits in that enzyme and slows the conversion. Less angiotensin two means less vessel tightening and less aldosterone.

What changes in the blood vessels?
Vessels widen. Pressure falls. The effect can start within hours, but the full drop on a monitor may take weeks. It works while you take it.

Dose and limits.
On the US label for adults with high blood pressure, the usual start is 10 mg once a day. Range is often 20 to 40 mg. Maximum is 80 mg. Do not use in pregnancy, or after a serious allergic reaction including angioedema. Watch potassium and kidney tests. Dry cough can occur.
