Cozaar is the brand name, and it may be the name you already know.

## 2. What is high blood pressure?

Hypertension means blood pressure that stays high.
You often cannot feel the number.
What changes is the trend on repeated readings.
Over years, that pressure strains the heart, the vessels, and the kidneys.
The label also covers reducing the risk of stroke in people with high blood pressure and a thickened left ventricle.
That benefit was not shown in Black patients in the cited trial.
Those are different plans.

Scene: a vessel under pressure. No drug. No face. Question header: What is high blood pressure? Source: label 1.1, 1.2, 14.2.

## 3. What signal tightens the vessel?

Angiotensin one is turned into angiotensin two.
The enzyme that does that is ACE.
Angiotensin two is a strong vessel tightener.
It also raises aldosterone from the adrenal gland.
Aldosterone changes salt and water.
That is the signal this drug interrupts.

Scene: angiotensin one (sand) and angiotensin two (coral) on a vessel wall. ACE is shown only as the converter, not as the drug’s target. Source: label 12.1.

## 4. What does the drug block?

Losartan is an angiotensin receptor blocker.
It blocks angiotensin two at the A T one receptor.
So does its active metabolite.
The receptor sits on vascular smooth muscle and on the adrenal gland.
There is also an A T two receptor.
It is not known to control blood pressure.
Losartan prefers A T one by about a thousand fold.
It does not block ACE.
It does not slow the breakdown of bradykinin.
That is the difference from an ACE inhibitor.

Scene: teal A T one receptor in a gray membrane. Coral angiotensin two approaching. Gold losartan seated in the pocket. ACE is off this picture. Labels in object colors. Source: label 12.1.

## 5. How does it sit in the receptor?

The parent drug is a reversible competitor at A T one.
The active metabolite is 10 to 40 times more potent by weight.
In binding studies it looks non-competitive.
Neither is a partial agonist.
Nothing is aiming.
The shape fits.
The block lets go for the parent.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

Scene: closer on the same receptor. Parent and metabolite labeled separately if both are shown. Do not invent atom contacts. Source: label 12.1.

## 6. What happens when that signal falls?

Vessels can relax, and the heart pumps against less resistance.
Aldosterone signaling falls too.
A lower pressure is the measured result.
It is not a count of blocked receptors in every tissue.
Some effect can show in the first week.
The maximum in the hypertension trials could take 3 to 6 weeks.
Feeling well does not mean it is cured.
It works only while it is taken.

Scene: the same vessel, now wider. No face. Source: label 12.2, 14.1.

## 7. How does it move through the body?

It begins as a swallowed tablet.
Food slows absorption and lowers the peak, but the amount absorbed is about the same.
The liver forms the more potent active metabolite.
The label does not name the enzyme that does this in these spoken lines.
About 14 percent of a dose leaves in urine as unchanged drug.
About 4 percent leaves as the active metabolite.
The rest leaves in feces.
It is not removed by hemodialysis.

Scene: tablet, then liver, then kidney. One picture per line. No invented transporter. Source: label 12.3.

## 8. What dose does the label list?

For adult hypertension, the label usually starts at 50 milligrams once a day.
If volume is depleted, as with a diuretic, the start is 25 milligrams.
The maximum is 100 milligrams once a day.
A person with mild or moderate liver impairment also starts at 25 milligrams.
These are label limits, not a dose chosen for you.
Pediatric dosing is a different plan.

Scene: three-line card. 50 mg start. 100 mg maximum. Label limits. Source: label 2.1, 2.3.

## 9. What are the limits?

There is a boxed warning for fetal harm.
When pregnancy is detected, stop it as soon as possible.
Do not use it after a serious allergy to losartan or to an ingredient in the tablet.
Do not use it with aliskiren in diabetes.
High potassium and a fall in kidney function can occur.
Those are the same-action risks of blocking angiotensin two.
Swelling of the face, lips, or throat needs urgent care.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources, and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
