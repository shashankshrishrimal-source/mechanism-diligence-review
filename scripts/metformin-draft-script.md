# Metformin (immediate-release tablets) MOA Script

Scope: US immediate-release metformin hydrochloride tablets (e.g., Glucophage). Not extended-release.

## Spoken Script

Welcome to Mechanism Diligence. You might be here to understand a drug you were prescribed, or to learn how it works. This one is metformin. Glucophage is a brand name you may know. This film is for education, not personal medical advice.

What is type 2 diabetes?
Type 2 diabetes means blood sugar stays too high. The liver can make too much sugar. The body may not respond well to its own insulin. You cannot feel the number early. Only a blood test shows it. Over years, high sugar can harm vessels, nerves, kidneys, and eyes.

How does metformin work?
Metformin is a biguanide. The label says it lowers blood sugar three ways. It decreases the liver’s production of new glucose. It decreases intestinal absorption of glucose. It improves insulin sensitivity so tissues take up more glucose. Insulin secretion does not increase.

Where does it act?
Most of the sugar-lowering happens in the liver. Liver cells normally release glucose into the blood. Metformin reduces that release. In the gut, less glucose is taken up from food. In muscle and other tissues, cells respond better to insulin and pull in more glucose. Blood sugar falls while the medicine is present.

What does the drop look like?
Fasting and after-meal sugar levels come down. The full effect on a blood test builds over days to weeks. It works only while you take it. It does not cure diabetes.

Dose and limits.
For adults with type 2 diabetes on immediate-release tablets, the usual start is 500 milligrams twice a day or 850 milligrams once a day, with meals. The dose can rise to a maximum of 2,550 milligrams a day, in divided doses. Do not use if the kidneys are severely reduced, or if there is metabolic acidosis. The serious risk is lactic acidosis, especially when the drug is not cleared by the kidneys. Watch kidney tests. This film does not show your personal risk.

For the questions and answers, the sources, and the references behind this film, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
