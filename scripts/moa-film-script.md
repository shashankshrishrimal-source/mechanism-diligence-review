# Mechanism Diligence — MOA film script

Locked on the ibuprofen film. Dark charcoal. One picture. Two narrations.

This is a shooting script, not a new idea for each drug. The camera, the cards, the colors, the order, and the sentence jobs stay. Only the drug, the players, and the cited facts change.

A film is educational. It is not personal medical advice, not a crystal structure, and not a dose chosen for one person.

---

## What never changes

**Frame.** 16:9, 1280×720. Background `#0B1218`. Cream type `#F3F0E8`. Muted type `#A8B2BA`. Teal `#4EC4BC` is always the body's machines. Gold is always this medicine. The raw material keeps one other color for the whole film. Soft studio light. No people, no blood, no brand-name logos, no watermark.

**Mark.** The real Mechanism Diligence logo, top left, turned white on the dark ground. Never redraw it. Beside it, every card: `Clear science. Informed decisions.`

**Words on the 3D.** None. The 3D plates are pictures only. Titles, subtitles, and molecule names are added after, in our type. Names follow the thing they name. Nothing sits on the play bar. Last line of type stays above the controls.

**Motion.** Camera almost still. One action per shot. Molecules do not swim on purpose. A blocker enters, holds, and — if the block is reversible — eases back. A scene holds until its narration finishes. Do not stretch a 10-second plate past about three times or the motion dies. If the words are longer than that, cut a clause, do not freeze the shot.

**Truth.** One claim per shot. If a label, a guideline, or a cited review does not say it, it does not appear. Do not invent onset, a milligram, or a contraindication. If a beat has no cited fact, cut the beat. Do not decorate the hole.

**Two tracks, one picture.** Scientific and lay use the same plates and the same order. The lay track changes the spoken words and the on-screen sentences. It does not change the biology, the numbers, or the pictures. A later language is the same swap: new voice, new sentences, same plates.

**Voice.** One calm adult voice for the whole film. Scientific track, a little slower than conversation. Lay track, shorter sentences, still an adult, never a cartoon. Define a hard word once, then use its name.

## The eight beats

| Beat | Card or picture | Job | Cut it if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Title | Card | Name, class, what it is for, what it does | Never |
| 02 Start | 3D | Where the story begins, and the raw material | The drug has no upstream step worth a shot |
| 03 Machines | 3D | The targets, what they make, and one useful job | Never, if a target is known |
| 04 Occupancy | 3D | How this drug meets the target | Never |
| 05 Consequences | 3D | The relief, and the harm from the same action | Never |
| 06 Timing | Card | Onset, duration, next dose | The label does not state them |
| 07 Limits | Card | Maximum dose and absolute do-not-use list | Never, if a US label exists |
| 08 Leaves out | Card | Personal risk, symptoms not cause, one thing it does not do, sources | Never |

Do not add a ninth beat. A second organ or a second pathway is a second chapter with the same eight beats, not a longer chapter one.

## Motion recipes

Pick one. Do not invent a fourth kind of movement.

**Reversible block.** The drug glides into the place, holds, then eases partly back out. The raw material waits outside and does not enter while the drug is seated.

**Irreversible block.** The drug glides in and stays. No ease-back. The line on screen says it stays.

**Selective.** Two machines are on screen. The drug enters only the named one. The other stays open.

**Not selective.** The drug is shown in both machines, or in one place labeled as both.

**Agonist.** The drug settles on the machine. The machine changes. The downstream signal increases.

**Antagonist.** The drug settles on the machine. The body's own signal approaches and does not take the place.

## Shooting script

Read `{SLOT}` as the filled word. The sentences around it do not get rewritten. Scientific narration is S. Lay narration is L. On-screen title is the short line. Subtitle is the second line.

### 01 — Title

Card. No molecule. Logo and tagline. Large `{DRUG}`. Under it, the class in one line. Three rows: Drug class, Uses, Effects. Footer: `Educational purposes only. Please consult a clinician.` Then: `Read the questions and answers for this drug.`

**S.** Welcome to Mechanism Diligence. This film explains how `{DRUG}` works, at the level of the cell. It is for education. It is not personal medical advice. `{DRUG}` is `{CLASS}`. It is used for `{USES}`. It works by `{EFFECT}`.

**L.** Welcome to Mechanism Diligence. This film shows how a medicine called `{DRUG}` works inside the body. It is here so you can understand it. It is not advice about what you, personally, should take. `{DRUG}` is `{CLASS}`. Adults use it for `{USES}`. It works by `{EFFECT}`.

### 02 — Where it starts

3D. `{START_PLACE}` fills the frame. `{WORKER}` is at the point of action. `{START_EVENT}` is labeled there. One piece, `{RAW}`, comes free and drifts. Nothing else enters.

Title: the lay or scientific one-liner for this beat, eight words or fewer. Subtitle names `{WORKER}` and `{RAW}`.

Labels, following the picture: the place, the worker, the event, the raw material.

**S.** When `{START_EVENT}` happens, `{WORKER}` acts at `{START_PLACE}` and releases `{RAW}`. That is the raw material for this pathway.

**L.** `{START_PLACE}` is where this starts. When `{START_EVENT}` happens, a worker called `{WORKER}` lets a piece go. That piece is called `{RAW}`. Think of it as the raw material for the alarm signals.

If beat 02 is not a membrane, keep the grammar. Name the place, the event, the worker, and the piece that moves. Do not add a second event.

### 03 — The machines

3D. `{MACHINE_A}` and `{MACHINE_B}` side by side, smaller than the frame, dark space around them. `{RAW}` moves toward a machine. `{PRODUCT}` leaves upward. If there is no second machine, one machine only. Do not invent a partner.

Title: what the machines make. Subtitle: both names, and that they do this job.

Labels: each machine, the raw material, the product.

**S.** `{MACHINE_A}` and `{MACHINE_B}` guide `{RAW}` through and make `{PRODUCT}`. Those signals lead to `{RELIEF}`. They also do a useful job: `{SAME_HARM}` depends on them.

**L.** Next, the machines go to work. Their names are `{MACHINE_A}` and `{MACHINE_B}`. They take `{RAW}` and turn it into messengers called `{PRODUCT}`. Those messengers help cause `{RELIEF}`. They also do useful jobs. `{SAME_HARM}` depends on them.

Say the useful job in the same breath as the harm it will explain later. Do not save it for a surprise.

### 04 — Occupancy

3D close view. One machine, or both if the drug is not selective. The place is open and readable. `{DRUG}` starts outside and follows the motion recipe. `{RAW}` stays outside while the place is taken.

Title: the binding in one line (`sits, then lets go` or `sits and stays`). Subtitle: selective or not, and that the machine is or is not permanently changed.

Labels: the place, the drug, the raw material. After the drug is seated: `Binds, then releases` or `Binds and stays`.

**S.** `{DRUG}` `{BINDING}` at `{PLACE}`. While that place is occupied, less `{RAW}` is converted. The machine is not permanently changed, unless the recipe is irreversible.

**L.** `{DRUG}` slides into `{PLACE}`. `{BINDING}`. While it is there, less `{RAW}` can get through. The machine is not broken. The block lasts only as long as the recipe says.

For an irreversible drug, the last lay sentence is: `This one stays stuck. The machine does not go back to normal on its own.`

### 05 — What changes

3D. A row of `{PRODUCT}`. As the narration speaks, some of them fade. Under them, one arrow each for the words in `{RELIEF}`. The arrows shorten. A line: `for a time`. The subtitle names `{SAME_HARM}` in the same frame. Do not show a happy patient. Do not show an injured organ.

**S.** With fewer `{PRODUCT}`, `{RELIEF}` can ease. The same change can reduce `{SAME_HARM}`.

**L.** When fewer messengers are made, `{RELIEF}` can quiet down. But the useful jobs get quieter too. `{SAME_HARM}` can weaken.

If the drug is an agonist, the row grows instead of fading, and the words are the effects that increase. The same-action harm is still in that frame.

### 06 — Timing

Card. Only if onset, duration, or next dose is on a label. Title: `When relief starts, and the next dose`. Rows: Onset, Duration, Next dose. Footer: `Follow the label or your clinician. This is not a personal schedule.`

**S.** `{ONSET}`. The effect usually lasts `{DURATION}`. If another dose is needed, the label typically spaces it `{NEXT}`. That timing is not a personal schedule.

**L.** For many people, `{ONSET}`. It usually lasts `{DURATION}`. If another dose is needed, the label usually says to wait `{NEXT}`. That is a general timing. It is not a schedule made just for one person.

Leave a row off rather than guess it.

### 07 — Label limits

Card. Two columns. Left, dosing. Right, do not use. Adults. Say which country's label. Footer: the source line, then `Not personal medical advice.`

Left, in this order: the store dose, the store maximum, the prescription maximum. Skip a line that does not exist.

Right: `{NEVER}`, one line each, four at most. `{ALSO}` in smaller type under them.

**S.** On the United States label, for adults, `{OTC_DOSE}`, and do not exceed `{OTC_MAX}`. The prescription maximum is `{RX_MAX}`. These are label limits, not a dose chosen for you. Do not use `{DRUG}` in these situations: `{NEVER}`. `{ALSO}`.

**L.** On the store label, for adults, the amount is `{OTC_DOSE}`. Do not go over `{OTC_MAX}`. If a doctor prescribes it, the highest amount on that label is `{RX_MAX}`. These numbers are limits on the label. They are not a dose picked for you. Some people should not use `{DRUG}` at all. `{NEVER}`. `{ALSO}`.

Read the never-list as separate short sentences. Do not soften a contraindication into "be careful."

### 08 — What this picture leaves out

Card. Title: `What this model leaves out`. Four lines:

1. Your personal risk.
2. It treats symptoms. It does not cure the cause.
3. `{NOT_THIS}`.
4. Sources: `{SOURCES}`.

Footer: `Educational purposes only. Clinical review is still pending.` Remove the pending line only after a named clinician signs the cut.

**S.** This model does not show your personal risk. `{DRUG}` treats symptoms. It does not cure the underlying condition. `{NOT_THIS}`. Sources include `{SOURCES}`. Independent clinical review is still pending.

**L.** This picture cannot tell you your own risks. The medicine calms symptoms. It does not cure the cause. `{NOT_THIS}`. The facts come from `{SOURCES}`. A clinician has not signed off on this film yet.

## Plates

Generate four pictures, then animate each once. No letters in the plate. No labels. No numbers. Solid charcoal ground. The enzyme does not touch the frame.

**Plate A, the start.** `{START_PLACE}`, `{WORKER}` at the point of action, one `{RAW}` coming free.

Motion: `{RAW}` lifts off and drifts. The worker stays. Camera almost still. Ten seconds. No new objects. No text.

**Plate B, the machines.** `{MACHINE_A}` and `{MACHINE_B}`, `{RAW}` at one opening, `{PRODUCT}` leaving the other.

Motion: the raw material eases toward the opening. The product rises a little. Machines stay. Ten seconds. No text.

**Plate C, occupancy.** One machine, the place open and wide enough to see inside. `{DRUG}` outside the opening. `{RAW}` off to the side, outside.

Motion: follow the recipe only. Ten seconds. The machine does not morph. No text.

**Plate D, the product.** A loose row of `{PRODUCT}` in the upper half. Empty dark space below for the arrows we add later.

Motion: a gentle drift. We fade members of the row in the edit. Ten seconds. No text, no arrows, no icons.

If a plate comes back with words baked in, paint them out or regenerate. Do not cover baked words with our labels.

## How to fill a new drug

1. Fill the slot table from the US label and one or two named reviews. Empty slots stay empty.
2. Pick the motion recipe. Write `{BINDING}` as one spoken clause that matches it.
3. Read both narrations aloud with the slots filled. If a sentence is false for this drug, change the slot, not the sentence around it.
4. Make the four plates. Approve them with no text before any motion.
5. Cut the scientific track first. Then the lay track on the same plates, with the on-screen sentences swapped to the lay lines.
6. A translation is a third pass. Same plates. Same facts. New voice. New sentences.
