## 2. What is excess stomach acid?

Excess acid can irritate the esophagus or the stomach lining.
You may feel heartburn or pain.
The label covers symptomatic GERD and erosive esophagitis from acid.
It also covers healing of duodenal ulcers.
Those are different plans.
Symptoms or an endoscopy confirm the need.
It is not a number you feel in the blood.

Scene: stomach and lower esophagus. No drug. No face. Question header: What is excess stomach acid?

## 3. What makes the acid?

Parietal cells in the stomach lining make the acid.
They have a pump on their secretory surface.
That pump is the H+/K+ ATPase.
It is called the proton pump.
It is the last step of acid production.
It works whether the stimulus is a meal or not.

Scene: parietal cell. Teal proton pump on the membrane. Icy acid leaving. No drug.

## 4. What does the drug block?

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor.
It blocks the H+/K+ ATPase.
The capsule is delayed-release because the drug is destroyed by acid.
It is absorbed after the granules leave the stomach.
It then reaches the parietal cell and sits on the pump.
The label says the binding is prolonged.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: teal pump in a gray membrane. Gold omeprazole seated. Less icy acid leaving. Labels in object colors.

## 5. What happens when the pump is blocked?

Basal and stimulated acid both fall.
The onset of the effect is within one hour.
The maximum is within two hours.
About half of the maximum effect is still present at 24 hours.
The inhibition can last up to 72 hours.
That is far longer than the time the drug stays in the blood.
The plasma half-life is half an hour to one hour.
That half-life is not how long the acid stays down.
It works while the medicine is taken.
It does not cure the underlying condition.

Scene: same parietal cell, less acid. No clock drawn as a measurement.

## 6. How does it leave?

The liver metabolizes most of the dose.
CYP2C19 does the larger share.
CYP3A4 does the rest.
Little of the parent drug leaves unchanged in the urine.
About 77 percent of a dose leaves in the urine as metabolites.
The rest leaves in the feces, through the bile.
The metabolites do not block the pump.

Scene: liver, then kidney, then gut. One picture per line. No invented transporter.

## 7. What dose does the label list?

For symptomatic GERD, the label lists 20 milligrams once a day for up to 4 weeks.
For erosive esophagitis, it lists 20 milligrams once a day for 4 to 8 weeks.
An extra 4 weeks may be given if healing is not complete.
These are label limits for these uses, not a dose chosen for you.
Take it before a meal.
Swallow the capsule whole.
Other uses, including H. pylori treatment and Zollinger-Ellison, are different plans.

Scene: three-line card. 20 mg once a day. Before a meal. Label limits.

## 8. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to omeprazole or to a related benzimidazole.
Do not use it with rilpivirine.
Less acid is the intended effect.
That same reduction can raise the risk of certain infections, including C. difficile diarrhea, and can lower absorption of some nutrients over a long time.
Long-term use has been linked to a higher risk of certain fractures.
