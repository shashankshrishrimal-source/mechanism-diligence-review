## 2. What is the nausea this film follows?

The label covers prevention of nausea and vomiting from chemotherapy, radiation, or surgery.
Chemotherapy can cause cells in the gut to release serotonin.
That serotonin can trigger the vomiting reflex through nerves and a center in the brain.
You feel the nausea.
This film does not cover every cause of nausea.

Scene: gut lining with enterochromaffin cells releasing coral serotonin. No drug. No face.

## 3. What does the drug block?

Ondansetron is a selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist.
It blocks the 5-HT3 receptors that serotonin uses to start the vomiting signal.
The receptors sit on nerves in the gut and in the area of the brain that triggers vomiting.
The label does not say which site is the only one.
It is not a dopamine blocker.
Nothing is aiming.
The shape fits.
The block lets go.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

Scene: teal 5-HT3 receptor on a nerve membrane. Coral serotonin approaching. Gold ondansetron seated.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

It begins as a swallowed tablet.
It is absorbed and reaches both the gut nerves and the brain center.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: tablet, then gut nerve, then brain center. One picture per line.

## 5. What changes?

Nausea and vomiting can be reduced in the labeled settings.
The effect continues while the medicine is present.
It does not treat the underlying cause of the nausea.
The plasma half-life is about 3 to 6 hours.
That half-life is not the duration of the antiemetic effect in every setting.

Scene: reduced signal on the same nerve. No clock drawn as a measurement.

## 6. How does it leave?

The liver metabolizes most of the dose.
Little unchanged drug leaves in the urine.
Confirm the exact urine fraction against the cited label before filming.

Scene: liver, then kidney. One picture. No invented percentage.

## 7. What dose does the label list?

For many adult uses the oral dose is 8 milligrams, or 4 to 8 milligrams.
For highly emetogenic chemotherapy a single 24 milligram oral dose is described.
These are label limits for the oral product, not a dose chosen for you.
The injection has different limits.
That product is a different film.

Scene: three-line card. 8 mg. 24 mg single oral for some chemotherapy. Label limits.

## 8. What are the limits?

Do not use it after a serious allergy to ondansetron.
Do not use it with apomorphine.
Do not use it in congenital long QT syndrome.
Blocking the 5-HT3 receptor can prolong the QT interval.
That is the same action that reduces nausea.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources, and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
