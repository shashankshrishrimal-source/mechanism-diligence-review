## 2. What is the condition this film follows?

Major depressive disorder means a low mood that lasts and interferes with life.
The label also covers obsessive-compulsive disorder, panic disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, social anxiety disorder, and premenstrual dysphoric disorder.
Those are different plans.
A clinician makes the diagnosis.
It is not a number on a blood test.

Scene: no drug. No face. Question header: What is the condition this film follows?

## 3. What does the drug block?

Sertraline is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor.
It blocks the reuptake of serotonin into the nerve.
That leaves more serotonin in the space between the nerves.
The label says this potentiates serotonergic activity in the central nervous system.
It does not name a second transporter as the main action.
Nothing is aiming.
The shape fits.
The block lets go.
The shapes are not the true shapes and are not to scale.

Scene: teal serotonin transporter in a gray nerve membrane. Coral serotonin held outside. Gold sertraline seated in the transporter.

## 4. Where does the work happen?

It begins as a swallowed tablet.
It is absorbed and reaches the brain.
The work is at the nerve endings that use serotonin.
We are changing the scale of the picture, not watching the medicine jump.

Scene: tablet, then brain outline, then the same synapse. One picture per line.

## 5. What changes?

More serotonin stays in the space between the nerves.
The clinical effect builds over days to weeks.
The plasma half-life is about 26 hours.
That half-life is not how long it takes for the mood to change.
Steady state is reached after about one week.
It works while the medicine is taken.
It does not cure the underlying condition.

Scene: same synapse, more coral serotonin outside the nerve. No clock drawn as a measurement.

## 6. How does it leave?

The liver metabolizes most of the dose.
The main metabolite is N-desmethylsertraline.
That metabolite is much less active and stays longer.
Hold: do not speak a recovery percent until the label sentence is pasted here. An earlier line said 40 to 45 percent of a dose is recovered in urine and feces over 9 days, and 12 to 14 percent of that is unchanged drug. “Of that” does not say whether the second percent is a share of the dose or of the recovered amount.
The label does not name one enzyme for the main path in these spoken lines.

## 7. What dose does the label list?

For adult major depressive disorder, the label starts at 50 milligrams once a day.
The dose may rise by 25 to 50 milligrams once a week.
The maximum is 200 milligrams once a day.
These are label limits, not a dose chosen for you.
Other labeled uses have different starts.
Mild liver impairment uses a lower start and a lower ceiling.
That plan is not this film.

Scene: three-line card. 50 mg start. 200 mg maximum. Label limits.

## 8. What are the limits?

There is a boxed warning.
Antidepressants increased the risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors in children and young adults.
Watch for worsening and for those thoughts, especially early and at dose changes.
Do not use it with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, or within 14 days of stopping one.
Do not use it with pimozide.
Do not use it after a serious allergy to sertraline.
More serotonin is the intended effect.
That same increase can cause serotonin syndrome if other serotonergic drugs are also used.
This film is for education, not personal medical advice.
Independent clinical review is pending.
For the sources, and to leave feedback, go to mechanismdiligence.com.
